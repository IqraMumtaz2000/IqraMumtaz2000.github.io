## Overview

This systematic review examines secondary immunodeficiency (SID) as an acquired and clinically significant condition associated with cancer therapies, chronic diseases, organ failure, nutritional deficiencies, and physiological aging.

The study explores how immune dysfunction can affect antibody production, T-cell function, and mucosal immunity, increasing vulnerability to infections.

## Research objectives

The review aims to:

- Examine therapy-driven risks and clinical patterns associated with secondary immunodeficiency.
- Explore functional antibody deficiencies and the limitations of relying exclusively on serum IgG measurements.
- Investigate inconsistencies in SID definitions, diagnosis, and clinical assessment.
- Review challenges surrounding immunoglobulin replacement therapy.
- Identify global gaps in standardized care and equitable access to preventive and therapeutic services.

## Methodology

The study followed PRISMA 2020 guidelines and used systematic searches of PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library.

The review identified and synthesized 100 studies across five clinical areas, covering evidence published between 2011 and 2026.

## Key findings

The review identified three major clinical outcomes associated with secondary immunodeficiency:

- Hypogammaglobulinemia.
- Reduced vaccine responses.
- Increased susceptibility to infections.

The findings highlighted several important issues:

- Clinically significant hypogammaglobulinemia was reported in approximately 20–50% of patients following anti-CD20 treatment.
- Patients receiving CAR-T therapy may experience prolonged immune deficiencies.
- Normal serum IgG concentrations do not necessarily exclude functional antibody deficiency.
- Diagnostic inconsistencies and unequal access to immunoglobulin therapy and preventive services remain important challenges.

## Research contribution

The review emphasizes the importance of functional immune assessment alongside quantitative immunoglobulin measurements.

It also highlights the need for internationally consistent diagnostic and management standards, improved clinical recognition, and more equitable access to appropriate care.
